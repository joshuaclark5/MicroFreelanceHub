## Legal And Liability Copy

Never write site copy, tool text, generated descriptions, template descriptions, profession page copy, SEO titles, meta descriptions, Open Graph text, schema data, or link-preview copy that promises legal protection, guarantees payment, guarantees enforceability, limits liability, prevents lawsuits, forces payment, or says the site creates legally binding protection.

Use neutral business-document language instead. Prefer phrases such as:

- "helps outline scope, pricing, approvals, timelines, and payment details"
- "organizes the terms both sides should review"
- "provides a starting point for a business agreement"
- "supports clearer expectations before work begins"

Avoid high-risk phrases such as:

- "legal protection"
- "guaranteed payment"
- "force payment"
- "limit liability"
- "legally binding"
- "prevent lawsuits"
- "enforceable contract"
- "protect yourself legally"

Protective legal wording belongs only in dedicated legal pages, Terms of Service, privacy/disclaimer content, or lawyer-approved copy. For all marketing, profession, template, builder, generated, metadata, and preview surfaces, keep the tone factual, modest, and non-lawyerly.

If database or AI-generated content includes legal-risk language, sanitize or replace it before rendering it anywhere user-visible or metadata-visible.
